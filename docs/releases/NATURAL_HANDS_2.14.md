# Natural hand guide · 2.14

The hand guide now uses a cupped playing shape, an articulated thumb and individually curved fingers. Unused fingers settle toward the hand instead of spreading beyond a neighbour. The palm opens for a wide chord; after release, it relaxes again. Compact keyboards use a shorter depth projection so the palm remains visible above the rail.

The artwork uses warm matte shading, soft joint creases and small nails. Palms stay opaque over the keys; only the wrist fades into the rail. The blue left-hand and honey right-hand numbers remain the teaching colours in every companion room. An idle finger number is hidden when it would cover an active instruction.

## Reading the guide

- Filled fingertip marks indicate the key being held.
- An outlined footprint shows the next destination while the hand moves.
- Finger numbers are 1–5 (thumb to little finger). Pitch numbers remain 1–7.
- The Hands button hides the illustration without changing playback or input.
- Reduced Motion keeps the pose still between note onsets.

The displayed fingering is a suggestion based on the score. The illustration does not measure the player's hand size, posture, tension or comfort.
